# Active Brownian Particle in a Harmonic Trap

A scientific computing project that simulates an **Active Brownian Particle (ABP)** confined in a two-dimensional harmonic trap using the **Euler–Maruyama method** for stochastic differential equations.

---

## Scientific Question

How does a self-propelled particle behave when confined by an external potential?

Active matter systems continuously consume energy and convert it into motion. Unlike passive Brownian particles, active particles exhibit persistent motion and operate far from thermodynamic equilibrium.

In this project, we investigate:

* The trajectory of an Active Brownian Particle (ABP)
* The competition between self-propulsion and confinement
* The steady-state spatial distribution of particles
* The emergence of ring-like structures in harmonic traps
* The differences between active and passive Brownian motion

---

## Governing Equations

The particle position is $\bf{r} = (x,y),$ and its orientation is described by an angle $\theta$. The overdamped Langevin equations are

$$\dot x = -\beta x + v_0 \cos\theta + \sqrt{2D_t} \eta_x(t),$$

$$\dot y =-\beta y+v_0 \sin\theta+\sqrt{2D_t} \eta_y(t),$$

$$\dot\theta = \sqrt{2D_r} \eta_\theta(t),$$

where

| Parameter   | Description                         |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| $v_0$       | self-propulsion speed               |
| $\beta$     | harmonic trap strength              |
| $D_t$       | translational diffusion coefficient |
| $D_r$       | rotational diffusion coefficient    |
| $\eta_i(t)$ | Gaussian white noise                |

The particle is confined by the harmonic potential

$$ U(x,y) = \frac{\beta}{2}(x^2+y^2),$$

which generates the restoring force

$$ \mathbf F = -\nabla U = (-\beta x,-\beta y)$$

---

## Model Assumptions

The model assumes:

* Overdamped dynamics (no inertia)
* A single active particle
* Constant propulsion speed
* Rotational diffusion of the orientation angle
* Gaussian white-noise fluctuations
* Isotropic translational diffusion
* Harmonic confinement
* No hydrodynamic interactions
* No particle-particle interactions

These assumptions correspond to the standard Active Brownian Particle model widely used in active matter physics.

---

## Numerical Method

The stochastic differential equations are solved using the **Euler–Maruyama method**, one of the most common numerical methods for Langevin dynamics.

The discretized equations are

$$ x_{n+1} = x_n + \left(-\beta x_n + v_0\cos\theta_n \right)\Delta t + \sqrt{2D_t\Delta t} \xi_x, $$

$$ y_{n+1} =  y_n + \left( -\beta y_n + v_0\sin\theta_n \right)\Delta t+ \sqrt{2D_t\Delta t} \xi_y,$$

$$ \theta_{n+1} = \theta_n + \sqrt{2D_r\Delta t},\xi_\theta, $$

where

$$ \xi_x,\xi_y,\xi_\theta \sim \mathcal N(0,1).$$

1. Define physical parameters

2. Initialize particle positions and orientations

3. Generate Gaussian random variables at each timestep

4. Integrate the stochastic equations using Euler–Maruyama

5. Store particle trajectories

6. Visualize:

   * orientation dynamics

7. Compare active and passive particle behavior

## Comparison with Passive Brownian Motion

For a passive particle

$$
v_0=0,
$$

the stationary distribution is

$$
P(x,y)
======

\frac{\beta}{2\pi D_t}
\exp\left(
-\frac{\beta(x^2+y^2)}{2D_t}
\right).
$$

The corresponding mean radius is

$$
\langle r\rangle_{\mathrm{passive}}
===================================

\sqrt{\frac{\pi D_t}{2\beta}}.
$$

The notebook compares this analytical prediction with the measured active-particle radius to quantify the influence of self-propulsion.

## Interpretation of the Results

Three competing physical mechanisms determine the dynamics:

### Self-Propulsion

The active velocity

$$
v_0(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
$$

drives persistent motion.

### Rotational Diffusion

The orientation angle evolves stochastically, causing the propulsion direction to decorrelate over time.

The persistence time is

$$
\tau_p
======

\frac{1}{D_r}.
$$

### Harmonic Confinement

The restoring force

$$
-\beta(x,y)
$$

pulls the particle toward the origin.

The observed dynamics result from the competition between these three effects.

Typical observations include:

* Persistent random walks
* Orbit-like trajectories
* Broadening of the particle distribution
* Increased mean radius compared with passive Brownian motion
* Ring-like accumulation for strongly persistent particles

These phenomena are widely observed in active matter systems such as self-propelled colloids, bacteria, catalytic Janus particles, and synthetic microswimmers.
